Options for the Future

The future of the gacha monetization model has a few options.

1: Banning gacha entirely

While this would eliminate the risk of exploitation and addictions, it presents a new problem. These games do need to be monetized in some way, and gacha seems to be the best existing model for long-lasting, large-scale RPGs. Banning gacha would mean increased prices on other in-game purchases, alternative monetization as laid out here, or needing to majorly scale back games. Lots of developers would likely lose their jobs due to budget cuts. Large-scale games would likely become more inaccessible, available only to those with money to burn. Game communities would be fractured. I don’t see the possible benefits outweighing the harm. This also violates the principle known as dignity of risk: the idea that people are entitled to reasonable risks. In this case, people are entitled to spend their money how they want. Banning gacha entirely may be the utilitarian option, but it violates the right to risk, disrupts established gaming communities, and could cause harm to developers.

2: Keep the gacha model as is

There are currently no legal regulations on this type of gambling: no age limits, spending limits, or required disclaimers. While it’s common practice (and often expected) for gachas to list exact rates, this is not required or enforced, and there have been a few scandals in which games have straight up lied about players' chances. This preserves the jobs of the developers as well as the rights of players to spend their money how they like, but leaves children and those dealing with gambling addictions vulnerable. While this respects rights, it does not consider the duties of creators or the safety of vulnerable communities.

3: Impose regulations on gacha

The biggest one is transparency: a legal requirement to declare all gacha rates, and legal protections for players who were lied to. Regulations could also include spending or age limits. For example, games directed towards children could have spending cutoffs, or a mechanism for required parent approval. I don’t think it’s realistic to simply erase the gacha mechanic from kid’s games entirely, for the reasons stated above, but as some of the most vulnerable consumers of gacha games, their safety needs to be prioritized. Another required regulation could be disclaimers that gacha is gambling, and information on the risks of it. This is my preferred option: it preserves dignity of risk, as well as fighting against the biggest cause of unhealthy gacha use, misinformation. Gacha games can be very harmful, and developers have the responsibility to mitigate that harm while still being able to create.
