The Whale Perspective

In the gacha community, players who spend lots of money on games (often $100+ a month) are known as “Whales.” As the amount spent increases, some earn nicknames such as Leviathans or Cthulus. There is of course a large “middle class” between free-to-play (F2P) players and Whales.

These players are the primary source of revenue for gacha games. They’re what let game companies continue to produce high quality games without mandatory charges. “Whale” is not a derogatory term: Whales are necessary for the gacha monetization model to work.

But is this behavior healthy? It depends. If someone has plenty of disposable income and love for a game, there’s nothing wrong with them spending money to both support its production and treat themself. The problem comes when this spending becomes reckless and compulsive. Gambling addictions are very easy to develop, and gacha is gambling. Limited-time availability and boosted rates encourage Whaling, and some games even have special prizes and exclusive separate gambling pools for use with paid currency.

With Whales as such a backbone of the community, it’s difficult to predict how possibly banning the gacha model would affect them. Putting aside the issue of monetization, some players may find themselves struggling to keep up, or finding they need to invest far more time in a game to maintain their enjoyment and skill.
